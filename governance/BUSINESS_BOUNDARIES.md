# PIE Business Boundaries

Status: Active

## PIE role
PIE is a remote technical-support function. PIE diagnoses, guides, recommends, confirms information, and maintains reusable support knowledge.

Physical actions on an agent/customer machine — repair, adjustment, reconnect/reseat, replacement, disassembly and similar on-site work — are performed by the agent/customer-side personnel unless a separately documented workflow explicitly states otherwise.

## Language consequence
Workbench and Knowledge content must distinguish:
- PIE recommendation/instruction;
- action actually performed by the agent/customer;
- observed result after the action.

A PIE instruction such as “replace/check/reseat X” must not be rewritten as “PIE replaced X” or as a verified fix unless the evidence records that the action was actually performed and the outcome is known.

## Replacement / warranty approval boundary
Replacement approval, warranty approval, financial responsibility and other service-commercial authorization are outside PIE authority unless a separately documented workflow explicitly delegates them.

When a partner's latest request is essentially asking whether replacement/warranty can be approved:
- first determine whether PIE still has a material technical action to provide;
- if the technical repair strategy is already clear, PIE should add an internal technical note with the technical recommendation and reasons;
- do not send a partner-facing PIE reply merely to approve/reject replacement or to restate that approval is outside PIE scope;
- route/transfer the ticket to the Service Manager or other authorized service owner for the approval/commercial decision.

For repeated-repair cases with confirmed internal water damage/corrosion, PIE may recommend complete-unit replacement from a technical repair-strategy perspective when further repair is poorly predictable or cannot reasonably ensure long-term reliability. This technical recommendation is not itself a replacement/warranty approval.

## Case vs knowledge
An ITR records what happened in a case. A Knowledge entry records a reusable, evidence-supported conclusion. A true case statement does not automatically qualify as reusable knowledge.
